Written by a human, for humans.

    I'm an immigrant, a hard worker, and someone who’s never taken an opportunity for granted.

    I grew up in a small city in southeastern Europe, where I learned early on to appreciate what I have and make the most of what comes my way. From flipping burgers at a local joint when I was 16 to working with some of the most talented people in the gaming industry, life has taken me places I couldn't have imagined back then.

    Along the way, I've had the chance to learn from people far more talented than me, work on things I'm proud of and call San Francisco home, a place where some of the most exciting things in technology and science are being built every day. I feel fortunate to have experienced all of this, and I try not to lose sight of that.

    I'm also a consumer. I love apps, games, tools and cool new gadgets. From something as simple as finding a parking spot to learning a new language, navigating taxes or opening a social app and somehow losing five hours of my day, I've experienced my fair share of what technology has to offer. I'm curious about the things people make, why they make them and what makes one product genuinely useful while another becomes just another icon on your phone.

    With AI changing the way we work and build, it's easy to get caught up in the technology itself. But I think the fundamentals remain much the same. We build things to entertain people, help them solve problems, express themselves or do something they couldn't do before. The tools will change, sometimes dramatically, but the people on the other side of what we make remain the same.

    That's something I try to keep in mind in my work. Whether I'm working alongside a team or building something for someone I've never met, I believe it's worth taking a moment to think about the people involved, what they need and how we can make their lives a little better. It's a simple idea, but one I keep coming back to.

    People first.

    My own path has been a bit of a winding one. I started in graphic and web design, moved into product design and eventually found my way into code. That opened up a whole new world for me. I could do more than design how something looked or felt. I could help build how it actually worked.

    That led me into creative technology and technical art, where I've had the opportunity to work on games, develop tools, build systems and bring ideas from concept to reality. These days, I'm working with AI pipelines, which feels like a natural continuation of a journey that's always taken me somewhere between design and engineering.

    In a world that's constantly changing, one thing I know for sure is that I'll never stop learning. I want to stay curious, keep exploring and remain excited about the things I haven't done yet. If there's one thing my path has taught me, it's that you don't always need to know exactly where you're going. Sometimes, you just need to be willing to learn, put in the work and see where it takes you.

    If you've made it this far, thank you for taking the time to read a little about me. I appreciate it, truly.

    Nenad M.
